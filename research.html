Structure-Based Drug Discovery • Antiviral Therapeutics

AI-Driven Discovery of Novel RdRp Inhibitors for Dengue Virus

Scientific Challenge

Dengue virus remains a major global health threat with limited antiviral treatment options. RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp) is a highly conserved enzyme essential for viral replication and represents a prime therapeutic target.

Computational Contribution

  • Target Structure Characterization: High-resolution modeling of the RdRp catalytic pocket and allosteric sites.
  • High-Throughput Virtual Screening: Screening extensive chemical libraries coupled with RDKit chemical similarity searches.
  • Molecular Dynamics & MM/GBSA: 100ns explicit-solvent simulations to evaluate trajectory stability and calculate binding free energies.

Key Scientific Findings

  • Prioritized promising lead compounds from multi-million chemical libraries.
  • Demonstrated that interaction stability and binding energetics (MM/GBSA) are more reliable indicators of antiviral potential than docking scores alone.
  • Identified promising lead scaffolds for further experimental optimization.
  • Computationally prioritized candidates were subsequently selected for enzymatic validation through collaborative research.
Scientific Significance: Demonstrates how combining virtual screening and molecular dynamics energetics drastically reduces false positives in antiviral discovery.
AI-Based Drug Discovery • Cardiovascular & Metabolic Target

Machine-Learning-Guided Identification of CETP Inhibitors

Scientific Challenge

Cholesteryl Ester Transfer Protein (CETP) plays an important role in lipid metabolism and has emerged as a potential therapeutic target in cardiovascular disorders. Understanding the molecular mechanism of CETP inhibition is essential for rational development of effective therapeutic candidates.

Computational Contribution

  • AI & Data-Driven Discovery: Trained high-accuracy machine learning predictive models (Prediction Score: 0.87) combined with pharmacophore mapping and fingerprint analysis.
  • Structural Validation: Full protein modeling, docking, and 100ns molecular dynamics simulations to track ligand stabilization.
  • Tunnel Region Analysis: Mapped hydrophobic interactions and hydrogen-bonding networks inside the CETP tunnel pocket.

Key Scientific Findings

  • Integrated machine learning and pharmacophore approaches to identify potential CETP inhibitors.
  • Evaluated experimentally characterized CETP variants using molecular modeling and simulation approaches.
  • Identified key residues contributing to ligand stabilization and inhibition mechanism.
  • Prioritized promising compounds showing sustained interactions within the CETP tunnel region.
Scientific Significance: Highlights an integrated AI and computational framework for discovering and optimizing candidates by combining predictive modeling with biophysical mechanistic insights.
AI/ML & Immunoinformatics • Precision Oncology

Machine-Learning Framework for Neoantigen Prediction

Scientific Challenge

Neoantigens derived from tumor-specific mutations represent promising targets for personalized cancer immunotherapy. Accurate identification of immunogenic candidates requires integration of antigen processing, presentation, and molecular recognition information.

Computational Contribution

  • Data Preparation: Mutation-derived peptide analysis and sequence-based feature extraction.
  • Deep Learning Prediction: Neural network models evaluating proteasomal cleavage, TAP transport, and MHC presentation affinity.
  • Structural Validation: High-precision Peptide-MHC modeling, molecular docking, and 200ns molecular dynamics simulations to quantify complex stability.

Key Scientific Findings

  • Developed an integrated computational framework for neoantigen prioritization.
  • Incorporated antigen processing characteristics including proteasomal cleavage considerations.
  • Combined machine learning predictions with structural validation approaches.
  • Evaluated molecular stability and interaction patterns using computational structural biology.
Scientific Significance: Connects cancer genomics and precision medicine by validating immunogenic epitopes through biophysical stability assessments.
Cancer Genomics & Proteomics • Dual-Target Molecular Modeling

Computational Investigation of Dual EZH2–EGFR Inhibition in Triple-Negative Breast Cancer

Scientific Challenge

Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) represents a major therapeutic challenge due to limited targeted treatment options and aggressive recurrence. Integrated analysis of disease-associated pathways and molecular targets can support the development of improved therapeutic strategies.

Computational Contribution

  • Genomic Analysis: TCGA patient cohort gene expression profiling and Kaplan-Meier overall survival correlation for EZH2 and EGFR co-expression.
  • Dual-Target Structural Modeling: Docking and all-atom MD simulations against both EZH2 (residues TYR661, ASP664, TYR677) and EGFR (residues MET793, LYS745, THR790) binding pockets.
  • Proteomic Interpretation: Differential protein expression mapping, functional enrichment, and cell cycle/DNA repair pathway modulation.

Key Scientific Findings

  • Identified the association of EZH2 and EGFR expression patterns with TNBC disease outcomes.
  • Supported rational design and characterization of a dual-target therapeutic strategy.
  • Computational analyses provided insights into molecular interactions and stability of the designed inhibitor.
  • Proteomic analysis revealed biological pathways associated with therapeutic response.
Scientific Significance: Demonstrates the power of multi-omics integration and dual-target structural modeling to de-risk next-generation oncology therapeutics.
